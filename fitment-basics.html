Fitment math you can use before buying

Use these rules alongside the vehicle-specific builds. A gallery photo is evidence that one combination worked on one car—not a blanket guarantee for every trim or every tire model.

How to use these examples: match the exact year/generation, trim and brake package first. Then confirm tire load/speed rating, approved rim-width range, hub bore, bolt pattern, lug seat/thread, brake clearance and inner/outer clearance. Test at full steering lock and with suspension compressed after installation.

Spacer guidance: measure before adding one

  • A spacer moves the wheel outward by its thickness and reduces effective offset by the same number of millimeters. Example: a +45 mm wheel with a 10 mm spacer behaves approximately like +35 mm effective offset for position calculations.
  • Do not use spacers to solve an unknown fitment problem. First check inner strut/control-arm clearance and outer fender clearance, and confirm the wheel still has proper hub engagement.
  • Use only application-correct hub-centric spacers and the correct longer fasteners or studs with the correct seat type and thread engagement. Follow the spacer and vehicle manufacturer torque instructions.
  • After lowering or lifting, get an alignment and recheck clearance under load, through steering lock, and during suspension travel.

Before you buy

There is no universal wheel spec for an entire make or model family. Confirm the exact model year, trim, brake package, wheel part number, and tire size. Specifications in this guide are decision steps, not a guarantee of compatibility. If any measurement is unknown, verify it with the vehicle or wheel manufacturer or a qualified installer before driving.

Wheel diameter vs. width

Wheel diameter is the nominal rim diameter (such as 17 inches); width is measured between the tire-bead seats, not across the outside lips. Tire makers specify an approved rim-width range for each tire size. Never assume a tire can safely be mounted on any width.

Offset (ET) in plain language

Offset is the distance between the wheel’s mounting face and its centerline. Higher positive offset generally places the wheel farther inward; lower positive offset or negative offset places it farther outward. The change in wheel position depends on both offset and width, so compare the full specs rather than offset alone.

Bolt pattern and center bore

Bolt pattern describes the number of fasteners and the diameter of the circle they form. Center bore is the opening that fits over the hub pilot. Both must be compatible. Correct lug-seat type and thread engagement are also essential; the wrong fasteners can damage parts or allow the wheel to loosen.

Tire size and overall diameter

A size such as 225/45R17 means nominal width 225 mm, aspect ratio 45% of that width for sidewall height, and a 17-inch rim. Approximate nominal diameter can be calculated as rim diameter plus twice the sidewall height converted to inches, but actual published tire dimensions vary by model and measuring rim. Compare manufacturer data, not just the size code.

Spacers and effective offset

A spacer moves the wheel outward. As a simple geometry estimate, effective offset equals wheel offset minus spacer thickness (in the same units). This only describes position; it does not establish safety. Hub engagement, fastener length/seat, wheel load, fender clearance, and applicable rules still must be checked.

A repeatable fitment workflow

  1. Identify exact year, trim, drivetrain, brakes, and suspension.
  2. Record the current wheel and tire specs from markings or documentation.
  3. Get candidate wheel specs: diameter, width, offset, bolt pattern, center bore, load rating, and brake template.
  4. Check tire maker approved rim width, load/speed ratings, and published diameter.
  5. Compare inner and outer clearance; account for steering lock and suspension compression.
  6. Confirm fasteners, torque, tire pressure, and TPMS requirements with manufacturer instructions.
  7. Have a qualified installer test-fit when any dimension or brake clearance is uncertain.

What calculators can and cannot tell you

A fitment calculator estimates geometry from the values you enter. It cannot inspect caliper shape, tire shoulder shape, fender liners, alignment, suspension travel, load ratings, or damaged parts. Treat results as a planning aid, not a guarantee that a combination is safe or legal.

Frequently asked questions

Is “flush fitment” the same as safe fitment?

No. Appearance does not prove brake clearance, load capacity, tire approval, or clearance through steering and suspension travel.

Can I use a wheel with a different center bore?

A larger bore may be usable with a correctly sized hub-centric ring if the wheel maker permits it. A smaller bore will not fit over the hub without proper machining by a qualified professional.

Should I trust an online fitment list?

Use it to shortlist options, then verify the exact vehicle, wheel part number, tire dimensions, brake clearance, fasteners, and manufacturer guidance.
