How to use these examples: match the exact year/generation, trim and brake package first. Then confirm tire load/speed rating, approved rim-width range, hub bore, bolt pattern, lug seat/thread, brake clearance and inner/outer clearance. Test at full steering lock and with suspension compressed after installation.

Spacer guidance: measure before adding one

  • A spacer moves the wheel outward by its thickness and reduces effective offset by the same number of millimeters. Example: a +45 mm wheel with a 10 mm spacer behaves approximately like +35 mm effective offset for position calculations.
  • Do not use spacers to solve an unknown fitment problem. First check inner strut/control-arm clearance and outer fender clearance, and confirm the wheel still has proper hub engagement.
  • Use only application-correct hub-centric spacers and the correct longer fasteners or studs with the correct seat type and thread engagement. Follow the spacer and vehicle manufacturer torque instructions.
  • After lowering or lifting, get an alignment and recheck clearance under load, through steering lock, and during suspension travel.

Before you buy

There is no universal wheel spec for an entire make or model family. Confirm the exact model year, trim, brake package, wheel part number, and tire size. Specifications in this guide are decision steps, not a guarantee of compatibility. If any measurement is unknown, verify it with the vehicle or wheel manufacturer or a qualified installer before driving.

Identify your Subaru precisely

Record model year, model, trim, drivetrain, brake package, and suspension modifications. WRX/STI brake and wheel-clearance needs can differ substantially from other Impreza trims, and specs can change between generations.

Confirm wheel and hub specifications

Check the exact bolt pattern, center bore, fastener seat/thread, wheel width and offset, and load rating from reliable vehicle and wheel documentation. Do not assume every Subaru shares the same bolt pattern or brake clearance. Spoke shape and barrel profile can cause caliper contact even when diameter appears sufficient.

AWD tire matching matters

On AWD Subarus, keep all four tires within the manufacturer’s permitted rolling-circumference and tread-depth differences. Mixing worn and new tires or combining different tire models/sizes can create drivetrain stress. Check the owner’s manual or Subaru service guidance for the exact limits; if one tire is damaged, ask a Subaru-qualified tire shop whether matching or replacing a set is required.

Tire diameter and gearing

Use the tire manufacturer’s published overall diameter and approved rim-width range. A larger diameter can affect speedometer readings, gearing, clearance, and electronic systems. Maintain appropriate load and speed ratings. Don’t choose a size based only on a forum photo or an online calculator’s “close enough” result.

Clearance checks

Check caliper/spoke clearance, inner clearance to strut and spring perch, outer fender/liner clearance, and contact at full steering lock and suspension compression. Lifted or lowered vehicles can have new clearance issues. After mounting, inspect all four tires for contact and ensure no tire is stretched beyond the tire and wheel manufacturers’ approved combinations.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run two new tires and keep two worn tires?

Do not assume that is safe for AWD. Follow the exact vehicle manufacturer’s circumference/tread-depth limits and get professional guidance.

Will a wheel fit all WRX years?

No. Confirm the specific generation, brake package, wheel geometry, and tire size.

Do spacers solve Subaru brake clearance?

A spacer may move the wheel outward but can create fender clearance and hardware issues. Verify the complete assembly and use properly engineered, compatible components.
